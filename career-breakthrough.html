// CAREER PERIOD // 2000–2009

Hollywood Breakthrough & Iconic Stardom (2000–2009)

Executive Summary: Between 2000 and 2009, Gerard Butler transformed into one of Hollywood's most bankable and versatile leading men. After landing title roles in Dracula 2000 and Attila, Butler gained worldwide acclaim for his vocal performance in The Phantom of the Opera (2004) before achieving global cultural icon status as Spartan King Leonidas in Zack Snyder's 300 (2006).

Dracula 2000 & Attila the Hun

Butler broke into American cinema with Wes Craven's Dracula 2000, bringing brooding charisma to the legendary vampire. He followed with the acclaimed television miniseries Attila (2001), showcasing his formidable physical intensity and commanding presence as the warrior chieftain.

The Phantom of the Opera (2004)

Director Joel Schumacher chose Butler to headline the lavish cinematic adaptation of Andrew Lloyd Webber's The Phantom of the Opera. Performing his own demanding operatic vocals alongside Emmy Rossum, Butler stunned audiences and critics with emotional vulnerability, cementing his leading-man status.

300 & The Spartan Phenomenon (2006)

Zack Snyder's 300 redefined action cinema and launched Butler into the Hollywood stratosphere. Grossing over $456 million worldwide, Butler's intense four-month physical conditioning, indomitable presence, and iconic battle roar "This is Sparta!" became permanent fixtures of global pop culture.

Versatility: P.S. I Love You & Law Abiding Citizen

Displaying remarkable range, Butler starred in the beloved romantic drama P.S. I Love You (2007) opposite Hilary Swank, Guy Ritchie's rock-and-roll underworld comedy RocknRolla (2008), and the gripping legal revenge thriller Law Abiding Citizen (2009), which launched his production company G-BASE.
